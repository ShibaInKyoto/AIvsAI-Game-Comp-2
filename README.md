# FATHOM

**A submarine descent roguelite. Light reveals. Sound betrays.**

A research station at the bottom of the deepest trench on Earth went silent.
Three weeks later its sonar beacon started pinging again, and something started
pinging back. You pilot the *Lantern*, a small salvage submersible, down through
five biomes of a trench that rearranges itself every dive, to find out what is
singing at the bottom of the world.

On the way down: hunters that track your light and your noise, three guardians
that each have one weakness to find, a trench that reshapes itself around you
when the Choir sings, the station crew's thirteen recovered logs, and eleven
wonders of the deep to witness, from a passing sperm whale to a lullaby sung
back from the dark. Every model, creature, cave, sound and note of music is
generated by code.

## Quick start (Windows 10/11)

1. Download `FATHOM-windows-x64.zip` from the [Releases](../../releases) page.
2. Extract it anywhere and run `FATHOM.exe`.

It is a single executable with everything embedded. It needs a GPU with Vulkan
or Direct3D 12, which covers any dedicated GPU from the last decade. The exe is
not code-signed, so SmartScreen may warn on first launch: choose
**More info > Run anyway**. Progress and settings are saved in
`%APPDATA%\FATHOM`. On first launch the title screen measures the frame rate
and lowers the graphics preset if your PC needs it (change it any time in
Settings).

## How to play

You descend through **11 zones**: two each in the Sunlit Shelf, Twilight
Reef, Midnight Caverns, Abyssal Vents and Hadal Trench, then the hadal floor
itself. Every zone is a procedurally generated cave cross-section. Find the
**descent** (the glowing fissure at the bottom) to go deeper.

* **Your battery is your life support.** It drains slowly all the time and
  faster as the motors work, the lamp burns and the sonar pings. Glow bulbs
  on the walls recharge it. Let it run dry and you have ten seconds of
  reserve to reach one before the life support fails.
* **Your lamp** lights the way and scans creatures for research. It is the
  battery's biggest drain, and some things hunt light.
* **Your sonar ping** charts the cave on your map, finds the descent and marks
  creatures through the dark. Everything with ears hears it.
* **Every movement makes noise.** The faint ring around the sub shows how far
  you can be heard. Lancers dash at sound, gulper eels feel vibration through
  the rock, and the Stalker hears everything. When it starts hunting you,
  you will hear your own heartbeat. Harpoon a creature before it notices you
  for an **ambush** that deals double damage. A **sonar decoy** sticks to the
  rock and pings on its own, drawing every hunter that hears it.
* **Thermal vents** in the deep vent fields rumble and glow, then erupt in a
  scalding column that shoves and burns whatever is inside, and roars loudly
  enough to bring hunters running. Creatures caught in it cook.
* **Loose stalactites** hang in the caverns. A ping, a boost or a blast nearby
  shakes them down onto whatever is below, and each one shatters loudly enough
  to bring down its neighbours. Harpoon one to drop it on purpose.
* **Salvage** comes from caches (hook them with the harpoon and reel them in),
  debris and creatures.
* Lose the sub and its salvage goes down with it: only the Salvage Insurance
  upgrade banks any of it (research is transmitted as you find it, so that
  is never lost). On your next dive its **wreck** lies in the zone where it
  sank: crushed, with half its cargo, if it imploded; dark but whole, with
  all of it, if its battery ran dry.
* Between zones you dock at a **decompression stop**: install one of three
  modules, repair the hull and recharge the battery for salvage, then
  *descend*, or *surface* and bank your salvage with a depth bonus. The dive
  report tells you what sank you and how to deal with it.
* Spend salvage in the **hangar** on permanent upgrades. Spend research (from
  scanning creatures and recovering logs) on three more submarines.
* In the second Sunlit Shelf zone a **sperm whale** passes through the blue
  behind you, eleven metres of it on slow beats of its flukes. Its clicks
  reach your sonar and chart the cave around it.
* On the Twilight Reef floor sit **giant clams** with glowing pearls inside.
  They snap shut in the light or at any loud sound: kill the lamp, wait in
  the dark for one to gape, and harpoon the pearl by its own glow. Do not
  swim into an open one.
* At the bottom of the Twilight Reef the way down is guarded by **the
  Lamplighter**, an angler the size of a whale that sees only light. Run dark,
  ping to break her lure's pull, then shine your lamp in her eyes up close (or
  feed her a flare) and strike while she is dazzled.
* In the Midnight Caverns waits a **glow-worm grotto**: a chamber whose ceiling
  hangs with thousands of glowing threads. They go dark at sound, the dark
  spreading out from every noise, and light again after a few quiet seconds.
  Drift through slowly and they light your way without the lamp.
* At the bottom of the Midnight Caverns the way down is sealed by **the Blind
  Mother**, a colossal eyeless eel that strikes at sound. Make noise beside a
  wall, slip aside, and hit her gills while her head is stuck in the rock.
* In the first Abyssal Vents zone a seam of **singing glass** seals off a
  chamber, a cache glinting behind it. Every sonar ping that reaches it rings
  it, a note higher if it was still ringing: three in a row, a rising chord,
  and it shatters. The Choir asks the same of you at the bottom.
* At the bottom of the Abyssal Vents walks **the Ironclad**, an armoured
  isopod the size of a bus whose plates turn every weapon. Only the vents in
  its lair crack its shell: hang above a vent and ping so it walks over to
  listen, wait for the eruption, and strike while the seams glow. It never
  goes looking for an eruption itself, and it rears up to slam anything that
  comes close.
* In the Hadal Trench **the rock moves**. Twice a zone the Choir sings and the
  trench rearranges itself around you: the tunnel behind you flows shut, and
  a new chamber opens ahead where there was solid rock, with something
  waiting in it. What you leave behind may not be there to go back to. And
  something answers your sonar the way it answered Meridian's: eleven seconds
  after a ping, your ping comes back up from far below, slowed and stretched
  into something like a word.

  ![The Choir sings and a new chamber flows open in the Hadal Trench, a cache waiting in it](docs/screenshots/trench.webp)

* In the last stretch of the Hadal Trench, a work lamp gutters out of a wall:
  **Vasquez's drone**, lost the night the trench closed, sealed into rock that
  flowed over it like wax. Come alongside to play its flight recorder and
  drain what charge is left in its cells.
* On the hadal floor, on the way down, stand the ruins of **Meridian Deep**
  itself. A few windows still have power, and its beacon still pings: each
  ping charts the cave around it and is loud enough to draw hunters. Over it
  hangs an audience of hunters held by the Choir's tone, facing the floor,
  perfectly still; each time the tone shifts they turn together. Slip past
  without touching one, or they all wake. Stay a while and the station's
  speakers play Okafor's lullaby, and something far below sings it back.
* At the bottom of the trench waits **the Choir**. It sings in waves. Answer
  each wave with a sonar ping as it reaches you (an answer costs no battery; a
  missed wave hurts and drains it), then harpoon its voice organs while they
  glow open.
* Quit whenever you like: the dive is saved at the start of every zone and
  **Resume dive** on the title screen picks it up again.

### Controls

| Action | Keyboard & mouse | Gamepad |
|---|---|---|
| Thrust | `W A S D` / arrows | Left stick |
| Aim lamp and tools | Mouse | Right stick |
| Harpoon (hold: out and reeling in; let go: it comes home) | Left mouse | RT |
| Use tool (flare / torpedo / decoy / shock) | Right mouse | RB |
| Cycle tool | `Q` | LB |
| Sonar ping | `Space` | A |
| Boost (loud) | `Shift` | LT |
| Toggle lamp | `F` | Y |
| Sonar chart | `Tab` | Back |
| Pick a module at a stop | `1` `2` `3` | D-pad + A |
| Pause | `Esc` | Start |
| Scroll hangar lists | Mouse wheel | Right stick |
| Fullscreen | `F11` | |
| Screenshot | `F12` | |

**Photo mode** (in the pause menu) freezes the dive, hides the HUD and lets you
pan, zoom and swing the camera round the cave before taking a picture.
Screenshots and photos are saved to `%APPDATA%\FATHOM\screenshots`.
Every keyboard and mouse control can be rebound under **Settings > Controls**
(prompts follow your keyboard layout, so WASD reads ZQSD on AZERTY).
**How to dive** on the title screen (and in the pause menu) sums up the rules
and controls. Settings include an **Assist mode** (40% less hull damage and
wider timing windows against the Choir), **Sound cues** (threat sounds such
as a lancer's wind-up, a vent's rumble or cracking rock flash a labelled arc
round the sub pointing at their source, and a Stalker's hunt shows as a
pulsing heartbeat, so the game is playable without sound), **Reduce
flashing** (bursts of light swell in softly and stay dim, shockwaves and the
damage flash are fainter, and moving rock glows without rippling),
screen-shake strength, gamepad rumble and four graphics presets. Opened from the title
screen, Settings can also erase all progress for a fresh start (two presses;
settings and key bindings are kept).

## Replayability

* A new trench every dive, plus a **daily dive** with a shared, date-based seed
  and a condition of the day (blackout, frenzy, currents...) in every zone.
  The title screen tracks your best result for today.
* Every dive ends with a **score** (depth, kills, ambushes, scans, logs and
  salvage, multiplied by pressure), and the title screen keeps your five best
  in **Records**.
* **Contracts** in the hangar: take a handicap for one dive (Glass Hull,
  Silent Running, Failing Lamp, Infested Waters, Weak Cells) in exchange for
  a bigger score and payout.
* 32 modules to draft into a build. Their stats stack; the pause menu lists
  what you have installed.
* 4 submarine frames: **Lantern** (balanced, flares), **Mako** (fast, torpedoes,
  fragile), **Bathysphere** (armored, long sonar, shock pulse), **Whisper**
  (near silent, quick scanner, sonar decoys).
* 12 hangar upgrades, a creature codex (hover a scanned species in the hangar
  to see it in the viewport) and 13 story logs to recover (find them all to learn
  what became of the crew). The hangar archive also lists the eleven **wonders**
  of the trench you have witnessed, each with a snapshot of the moment you
  first saw it, beside the logs that told of them, and where to look for the
  rest.
* Twelve creature species with their own senses and tells: shelf mantas, gentle
  giants wider than the sub that glide through the sunlit zones with a school
  sheltering under their wings, drifting jellies,
  dashing lancers that hunt the shoals, spined puffers that swell into living
  mines when rushed, angler-like lurelights, burrowing gulper eels,
  wall-crawling isopods, stinging siphonophore chains, caches that turn out to
  be mimics, shoals, ghostfin packs that hunt the lamp on the hadal floor, and
  the Stalker, which hunts by sound across the whole zone.
* 17 milestones (silence the Choir, scan every species, kill a Stalker...), each
  unlocking a hull paint.
* **Zone objectives** for bonus salvage or research: open caches, hunt, scan
  species, stay silent, harvest bulbs, recover a log.
* **Zone events**: strong currents, blackouts, feeding frenzies, wreck
  fields (the broken hulls of an earlier expedition, salvage spilled around
  them), whale falls (a great skeleton on the floor, glowing with bone worms
  and crowded with feeding fish), dead calm and bioluminescent blooms. Also:
  silt storms (everything, you included, sees half as far), magnetic anomalies
  (sonar reaches half as far, the descent compass wanders), migrations
  (schools streaming down the main tunnel with lancers on their tails),
  tremors (every half minute the zone shakes and rock comes down from the
  ceilings), warm layers (your cells drain slower, everything else moves
  faster) and jelly blooms. The next zone's event shows in the forecast at
  each decompression stop.
* **Pressure levels** unlock after you beat the Choir. Each level stacks a new
  modifier on top of the ones below it.

## Under the hood

FATHOM is built with **Godot 4.5** (Forward+ renderer) in statically typed
GDScript. All 3D art is generated at runtime from code. The design document
and an architecture overview are in [docs/DESIGN.md](docs/DESIGN.md).

* **Caves** (`src/world/level_generator.gd`). The generator carves tunnels,
  chambers and shafts into a signed distance field with smooth-min SDF
  operations. Domain-warped noise then erodes the walls and opens organic
  caverns. A corridor wide enough to pass comfortably is then re-opened along
  the route, a flood fill marks the water the sub can reach, and pockets it
  can't reach are sealed. Generation runs on a worker thread in under half a
  second.
* **One field for everything** (`src/world/terrain_field.gd`). The same SDF
  drives collision (circle vs field), creature steering, projectile ray
  marching and rendering. It is uploaded as a half-float texture, and a vertex
  shader extrudes flat chunk grids into a relief (`terrain.gdshader`), with
  triplanar texturing, sediment, caustics and veins. Walls are pulled onto the
  exact collision outline, so what you see is what you hit.
* **Sonar** (`sonar.gdshaderinc`). Pings are global shader uniforms. Terrain,
  props and creatures all draw the wavefront and a fading contour map.
* **Creatures** (`src/creatures/`). Each species is a small state machine on a
  shared base: senses (hearing, light, line of sight), steering and wall
  avoidance. Their bodies are lathe meshes animated by a shared swim shader;
  shoals are boids on a MultiMesh.
* **Audio** (`tools/audio/`). Every sound effect and every note of music is
  synthesized by a numpy script: FM bells, detuned pads, formant "choir"
  voices, convolution reverb and seamless loops.

### Repository layout

```
src/
  autoload/   settings, signal bus, audio mixer, save/progression, scene router
  core/       run state, meta progression, submarine stats
  data/       biomes, modules/upgrades/frames, codex, logs, zone events, pressure
  world/      generator, terrain field + renderer, level, props, pickups, interactables
  creatures/  creature base, twelve species, three lair guardians and the Choir
  player/     submarine, model, harpoon, tool belt, projectiles
  dive/       dive controller, camera, tutorial, test autopilot
  ui/         theme, HUD, sonar chart, menus, hangar, decompression stop, ending

```

### Building from source

Requires Godot 4.5.1 with export templates installed.

```sh
godot --headless --import                                       # first-time import
godot --headless --path . -s res://tests/run_tests.gd           # 148 tests: generation, traversal, events, hazards, bosses, frames, saves, assets
godot --headless --path . res://tests/flow_test.tscn            # end-to-end menu and dive flow
godot --headless --export-release "Windows Desktop" build/windows/FATHOM.exe
python3 tools/audio/generate.py                                 # regenerate all audio (numpy + ffmpeg)
```

Debugging helpers: `-- --autopilot --zone=4 --frame=whisper --shots=<dir>` flies
a bot through a zone (it uses tools too) and saves screenshots. `-- --capture=<dir> --capture-at=5` screenshots
any screen. `godot --headless --fixed-fps 20 --path . -- --autopilot --time=900`
soak-tests whole dives far faster than real time.

CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) lints and checks every script, runs the tests
(failing on any runtime script or shader error, via `tools/ci/godot_strict.sh`) and
exports the Windows build on every push. `release.yml` publishes a GitHub
release.
